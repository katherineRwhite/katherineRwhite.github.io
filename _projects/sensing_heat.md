## Overview

The Sensing Heat project is an interdisciplinary community-based study examining
inequitable indoor heat exposure among older adults in Vancouver. Using a
mixed-methods design, we combine continuous indoor temperature monitoring with
qualitative methods including interviews and weather journals to understand how
housing conditions, neighbourhood factors, and socioeconomic circumstances shape
individual heat exposure.

## Methods

- Continuous indoor temperature sensors installed in 17 older adults' homes
- July–September 2025
- Daily weather journals and in-depth interviews

## Partners

- South Vancouver Seniors Network
- Vancouver Coastal Health
- City of Vancouver

## Key Findings

- Same-day indoor temperatures differed between participants by as much as 10°C 
  despite shared outdoor weather conditions
- 10 of 17 participants reported very hot indoor conditions on at least one journal 
  day, ranging from 2–80% of days across individuals
- Structural factors, including building features, dynamics with landlords and 
  building managers, and neighbourhood characteristics, shaped indoor temperatures 
  in ways that objective measurement alone could not capture
- Access to real-time temperature data supported participants in making informed 
  decisions about cooling strategies
